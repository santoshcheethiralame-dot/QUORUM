## 1. How the system is laid out

```
 FREE DATA SOURCES
        │  adapters (one per source, with fallback + quality checks)
        ▼
 RAW STORE (files, never overwritten)  ──►  CLEAN STORE (point-in-time tables)
        │
        ▼
 FEATURES ──► STRATEGIES (each outputs a forecast, never an order)
        │
        ▼
 SLEEVES (groups of strategies, each with a risk budget)
        │        ▲
        │        └── REGIME GATE (scales sleeves 0 to 1)
        ▼
 COMBINER (one net target position per instrument)
        ▼
 RISK OVERLAY (limits, drawdown brake, exposure caps)   ← always has the last word
        ▼
 TICKETS + PAPER LEDGER ──► DASHBOARD, PHONE BRIEF, JOURNAL
```

One book, one net position per instrument. That single rule is what stops strategies from fighting each other. Section 4 covers the detail.

## 2. Interface: how you use it

### Your day (IST)

| When | What arrives | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| 07:30 | Morning brief on your phone: overnight US and crypto, regime light, India tickets, anything contested | Read (2 min). Mirror India tickets by hand if you want |
| 18:30 | Pre-US brief: US tickets for tonight's open, risk status | Read. Nothing to click; the paper ledger fills itself at the open |
| Any time | Alert only if a limit trips or a position needs attention | Open the dashboard |
| Sunday | Weekly review: profit by sleeve, paper vs backtest, signals gaining or losing accuracy | Approve or reject promote/demote proposals |

### A ticket (the unit of "what to do")

```
BUY  QQQ   18 units  (2.1% of book)      limit: yesterday close + 0.2%
Why:   trend +14, ranker +6, reversion -5  →  net +9  (scale -20..+20)
Risk:  adds 0.3% to daily volatility; AI-theme exposure 22% of cap 30%
Exit:  net forecast below +2, or book drawdown brake
Valid: today's open only
```

Every ticket shows which strategies voted which way. Disagreement is visible instead of hidden.

### Dashboard pages

| Page | Shows | Question it answers |
|---|---|---|
| **Today** | Regime light, tickets, alerts | What do I do now? |
| **Book** | Positions, exposure by asset / sector / theme, profit by sleeve, drawdown gauge | What do I hold and why? |
| **Signals** | Vote matrix: instruments × strategies, net forecast, "contested" flags | Who agrees, who disagrees? |
| **Radar** | Chip cycle, AI trade, macro, geopolitics, flows: each indicator with trend and last update | What is changing in the world? |
| **Journal** | Every ticket, fill, override and its outcome; paper vs backtest tracking | Is the system doing what the research said? |
| **Lab** | Backtests and candidate strategies awaiting promotion | What is next in the pipeline? |

### Three operating modes

1. **Shadow**: system trades its paper ledger alone. You watch.
2. **Advisor** (default once it is stable): tickets go to you; you mirror what you choose and log overrides. The ledger tracks both "system" and "you" so you can see whether your overrides help.
3. **Auto-paper**: orders go to a broker's paper API. Optional and only where a free paper API exists.

### Laziest build that works

- Dashboard: a static HTML page regenerated by each scheduled run. No server, no database to host.
- Phone: a Telegram bot message with the brief and a link.
- State: the paper ledger is a file in the repository, so history is versioned for free.
- Upgrade to an interactive app only if the static page proves limiting.

## 3. Live data at $0

### Honest limits of free data

- No consolidated real-time US tape. Free real-time is one exchange (IEX), a few percent of volume. Fine for daily decisions, wrong for intraday microstructure.
- Options data is delayed or indicative.
- Unofficial scrapers break without notice, and some sites block cloud data-centre addresses (NSE is known for this, memory).
- Free tiers get cut. Example: Oracle's free ARM server allowance was halved in August 2026.
- Free language-model tiers may use your prompts for training.

So every source sits behind an adapter with a fallback, raw responses are stored forever, and a daily check flags stale or missing data before any strategy runs.

## 4. Making strategies work together

### The core idea

Strategies never place orders. Each one outputs a **forecast** per instrument on a common scale from -20 to +20, calibrated so its average absolute value is 10. This is Robert Carver's framework from *Systematic Trading*, which is public, simple and built for exactly this problem.

Two strategies disagreeing is then not a contradiction. Trend at +14 and reversion at -5 nets to a smaller long. The size of the position expresses the confidence.

### Layers

| Layer | What happens | Rule that prevents conflict |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Strategy | Forecast per instrument, tagged with a horizon (fast / medium / slow) | Common scale; capped at ±20 |
| 2. Sleeve | Strategies of one kind are averaged with fixed weights, then multiplied by a diversification multiplier | Weights are set by hand from correlations, not optimised (optimised weights overfit) |
| 3. Regime gate | A 0-to-1 multiplier per sleeve | The gate scales, it never votes on direction |
| 4. Combiner | Forecasts become positions through volatility targeting, then are summed per instrument | One net position per instrument; no sleeve holds its own inventory |
| 5. Risk overlay | Limits applied to the net book | Can only reduce risk; overrides everything above |
| 6. Buffer | Trade only if the target differs from the current position by more than about 10% | Stops fast signals from churning the book |

### Proposed sleeves

| Sleeve | Contents | Horizon | Starting risk share |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Trend and carry | Multi-horizon trend on ETFs/futures proxies and crypto; carry where measurable | Slow | 30% |
| B. Stock ranker | Boosted-tree cross-sectional ranking on liquid stocks, market-neutral or long tilt | Medium | 25% |
| C. Short-term reversion | RSI(2) / bar-strength / reversal family on index ETFs | Fast | 15% |
| D. Volatility premium | Small defined-risk option-selling proxy | Medium | 10%, gated |
| E. Events and calendar | Turn-of-month, index rebalances, earnings drift, lock-ups | Event | 10% |
| F. Crypto carry | Funding and basis capture | Medium | 5% |
| G. Thematic radar tilt | Chip-cycle and AI-trade dashboard readings as slow tilts | Slow | 5%, capped |
| H. Tail hedge | Convexity when it is cheap | | Cost budget, not risk share |

Shares are a starting point for paper trading, not a recommendation.

### Position sizing, in one line

position = (net forecast ÷ 10) × (sleeve risk share × target book volatility × capital) ÷ instrument volatility

Then sum across sleeves per instrument.

### Rules for real conflicts

1. **Horizon ladder.** Slow sleeves set the core position. Fast sleeves have their own small budget, so they can trim or add around the core but cannot flip it.
2. **Gates are multipliers.** Example: the volatility-premium sleeve goes to zero when the volatility curve inverts; reversion is scaled down in high-volatility trending regimes; trend is scaled up.
3. **Hedges come last.** The hedge is computed on the net book after all alpha is combined, so a hedge never fights a signal.
4. **Theme and factor caps.** Net exposure to market beta, to any sector, and to the AI theme is measured and capped at the book level. Two sleeves that are both secretly "long AI" get caught here.
5. **Drawdown brake.** Book risk is halved at a first loss threshold and cut to zero at a second, whatever the signals say.
6. **Event blackout.** Named sleeves stand down around scheduled events (earnings for the reversion sleeve, central-bank days for premium selling).
7. **Discrete trades.** Event trades take fixed risk units from sleeve E's budget, so they are not netted away but still respect the caps.
8. **Options.** Their delta is folded into the same net position per underlying; vega and gamma have separate limits.
9. **Contested flag.** If two sleeves disagree strongly and net to near zero, the dashboard marks the instrument "contested". Information for you, not an action.

### Keeping the set healthy

- **Correlation check before admission.** A new strategy whose returns correlate above about 0.7 with an existing one is merged or dropped, not added.
- **Attribution.** Each sleeve gets a virtual profit line (its share of each net position times the return, with costs shared by turnover). You can see who earns and who costs.
- **Promotion ladder.** Backtest → 8+ weeks in Shadow at zero weight → small weight → full weight. Demotion when paper results fall outside the band the backtest predicted.
- **Meta-layer later, not now.** Fixed hand-set weights first. A learned weighting by regime comes only after months of paper history exist to train it on.

## 5. Build order

| Phase | Delivers | Done when |
|---|---|---|
| 0. Skeleton | Repository, data adapters for prices and macro, raw and clean stores, quality check | A scheduled run pulls and validates data daily |
| 1. First sleeve end to end | Sleeve A (trend), combiner, risk overlay, paper ledger, static Today and Book pages, phone brief | You receive a real ticket and the ledger tracks it |
| 2. Second and third sleeves | Sleeves C and E; Signals page with vote matrix and contested flags | Netting visibly works on a day when sleeves disagree |
| 3. Ranker | Sleeve B with purged validation; Lab page | Paper results track the backtest band |
| 4. Radar | Chip, AI, macro, geopolitics dashboards feeding sleeve G | Radar page updates on schedule |
| 5. Extras | Volatility sleeve, crypto carry, India tickets, weekly review | Each passes the promotion ladder |

Each phase ends with something you can use. Stop at any phase and it still works.

## 6. What this plan does not do

- No live money. No broker order automation in India.
- No intraday or speed-dependent strategies in the first version.
- No promise of profit. The base rates in the street-methods file apply to us too.
